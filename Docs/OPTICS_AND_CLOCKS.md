# Stellar objects and observer physics boundary

**Current scope update (2026-10-05):** the user subsequently authorized an approximate bending-only demonstration and deferred redshift. Live common-plane thin-lens stellar images are now implemented; see [approximate lensing](APPROXIMATE_LENSING.md). The unavailable-provider and dataset-backed discussion below refer to the future full relativistic renderer and clocks, not a blocker on the approximation. The approximate lens map does not generate clock readings or redshift.

## Implemented foundation, not a relativistic optical simulation

The earlier starfield was already procedural geometry, not a bitmap. It followed the camera and represented an infinitely distant background. It is replaced with **1,400 deterministic `StellarSource` GameObjects** at fixed, finite exhibit positions, with stable source IDs, authored rest tints and display radii. `StellarSourceField` renders their camera-facing point images in one shared dynamic mesh rather than introducing 1,400 draw calls. Moving the observer produces geometric parallax; it never translates the source field. Source positions and apparent image geometry are separate concepts, so a later lensing renderer can produce multiple images of one emitter without moving the emitter itself.

These are synthetic sources, not a measured astronomical catalogue. Distances of 85–125 exhibit units, point sizes and rest colours are authored for visibility. They are **not** physical stellar distances, radii or calibrated spectra. Finite-distance parallax is therefore a display effect, not an astronomical prediction. Current point images use the optional approximate thin-lens map and remain spectrally unshifted. Neither changing head angle nor moving in Unity is silently interpreted as relativistic redshift.

## Shared optics and clock provider

`ObserverPhysicsBridge` lives on the visitor camera. `IObserverPhysicsProvider` is the future integration boundary for both backwards light-ray tracing and clock comparison; a provider must own a consistent spacetime model, display-to-metric mapping, physical time, source definitions and observer worldline. `ObserverViewRequest` explicitly contains a **display pose**, not an SI position or four-velocity. The installed `UnavailableObserverPhysicsProvider` is labeled staging and returns unavailable values, not artificial shifts or clock readings.

`TracePastDirectedRay` describes a ray from the observer into the past. A future optical renderer will sample a field of rays; several rays may reach the same source, providing multiple lensed images. Its `StellarRaySample.FrequencyRatio` convention is `nu_observer / nu_emitter`, with `z = 1 / ratio - 1`: positive z is redshift and negative z is blueshift. Captured rays have no observed source frequency. This interface is not an implemented integrator or a stereo renderer.

`RelativityClockSnapshot` carries **complete** visitor and reference proper-time totals (seconds), and a visitor-to-reference local ticking-rate ratio. It does not accumulate Unity frame time. The reference is an ideal observer in the asymptotically weak-field region, not a literal clock entirely outside all gravity. A future model must state its reference worldline and synchronization convention. Clock comparison includes kinematic as well as gravitational time dilation; a measured photon frequency ratio is not generally interchangeable with a clock-rate ratio for arbitrary moving observers.

Model ID, epoch and observer-worldline revision guard the snapshots. Seek, reset, scenario changes and applying a new starting separation invalidate the epoch. Viewpoint teleports, including body focus and orbit reattachment, invalidate the observer worldline. Ordinary head rotation and continuous movement do not reset it. A provider must reconstruct or deliberately restart a physical history after invalidation; complete totals prevent duplicate accumulation. Changing providers invalidates the epoch. Playback speed changes traversal speed, not the spacetime or local proper-time rate. A future attached wrist/body clock can call the same `CaptureClocks()` method as the spatial comparison card; it must not start an independent clock simulation.

The information area includes an **Observer clock comparison** card. It shows visitor/reference clocks and their difference as **Not connected / unavailable**, alongside a warning that approximate bending adds no clock rate. **K approaches the clock comparison** directly; F returns to the exhibit. It hides with the other information cards on opening the learning board or hiding the partner interface. No fake numerical clock runs in the normal scene.

## Selected scope: dataset-backed binary optics

The scene's moving binary and glowing disks remain a storyboard; the separately staged leading-order inspiral is not a binary spacetime metric. Full strong-field bending, shadow/multiple images, spectral shifts and lensing magnification require null-geodesic ray tracing through a suitable metric, with emitter/observer four-velocities. Frequency is measured by contracting photon momentum with observer four-velocity. Incoming distant starlight can be **blueshifted** for an observer deep in a gravitational well; it must not always be painted red. Mere head rotation changes the viewing direction, not the observer's energy or proper-time rate.

The user initially selected **dataset-backed binary optics** on 2026-10-05, then explicitly authorized a bending-only approximation for the exhibit. The new thin-lens demonstration is not completion of that larger research stage. For a future accurate binary renderer, obtain suitable time-resolved numerical binary-spacetime data and develop dataset-backed ray tracing. Waveform data alone is insufficient. Validate metric coverage, coordinate/tetrad conventions, emitter spectra, numerical convergence, time interpolation, motion/Doppler effects, per-eye rays and hardware performance before calling the result accurate or VR-ready.

Adding two single-black-hole redshift factors or screen warps does not solve the binary spacetime. No numerical-relativity dataset, physical scene mapping, light integrator, spectral renderer or headset performance measurement has been introduced in this foundation change.

Primary references: [Bohn et al., binary strong-field ray tracing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.7775), [Riazuelo, Schwarzschild observer optics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06025), and [Einstein Toolkit binary simulation example](https://einsteintoolkit.org/gallery/bbh/index.html).

## Metric-based measurement code added

`AdmObserverPhysics.cs` is pure double-precision C# included in Unity and linked into the source test project. Given a valid 3+1 metric sample (lapse, shift, all six spatial-metric components) and a coordinate velocity in matching geometrized units, it calculates the local proper-time rate from the timelike metric contraction. Given null future-directed photon momentum, it measures energy using the same metric and normalized observer velocity. Endpoint energy ratios supply the frequency ratio. It checks positive-definite spatial slices, finite values, positive lapse, timelike observers and the photon null constraint.

This is usable **local measurement mathematics**, not a ray tracer or binary dataset provider. It does not determine how a photon reaches the observer, calculate metric derivatives, integrate observer history, extrapolate an absent metric or map Unity motion into a physical trajectory. Tests cover Minkowski rest/motion, approaching/receding Doppler shifts, non-unit lapse, nonzero shift and a non-diagonal spatial metric. The fixtures are analytic tests, not binary data or a simulated numerical-relativity result.

## Dataset research result and current blocker

Checked the [Einstein Toolkit example](https://einsteintoolkit.org/gallery/bbh/index.html) and its [Zenodo record 155394](https://zenodo.org/records/155394). The public release is a subset with sparse 3D output; the full output is several terabytes and available by request. Crucially, its [published parameter configuration](https://zenodo.org/records/155394/files/GW150914.rpar?download=1) lists selected conformal/curvature quantities for 3D output, not the complete lapse/shift/spatial-metric history required here. This public subset has **not** been accepted as a ray-tracing dataset; the archive was not downloaded.

[SXS file documentation](https://data.black-holes.org/waveforms/documentation.html) distinguishes waveform/horizon records from the initial metric-related fields in `EvID`. Initial fields do not supply a continuously evolving binary spacetime. These sources establish promising provenance and research contacts, but do not give this project a verified, ready-to-render metric history.

The future full relativistic provider is blocked on **data access and suitability**, not the user's physics choice. That provider cannot be activated until a real export is inspected. This does not block the current approximate bending demonstration. See [data request and acceptance specification](NUMERICAL_SPACETIME_DATA_REQUEST.md). No external request was sent and no large dataset was downloaded.
